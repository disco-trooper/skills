# Audit Workflow

## 1. Intake

Capture:

- company context,
- business goal,
- candidate processes,
- systems involved,
- stakeholders,
- urgency,
- what has already been tried,
- current decision owner.

Classify the situation:

| Situation | Recommended next step |
| --- | --- |
| One clear process, unclear details | Single-process audit |
| Many candidate processes | Roadmap audit |
| Clear process, clear data, clear owner, clear success metric | Pilot proposal |
| No owner or no data | Bounded discovery if small; paid diagnostic if the organization is messy or the work will require analysis |

## 2. Scoping Call

Goal: decide whether a paid audit/pilot makes sense.

Allowed free outputs:

- questions,
- missing-data list,
- recommended next paid step.

Avoid free outputs:

- process map,
- target architecture,
- implementation plan,
- vendor comparison,
- detailed automation design.

If no owner or no data exists, do not automatically keep working for free. Use a short free scoping step only to decide whether the next step is a paid diagnostic.

## 3. Paid Audit Structure

### Inputs

- sample documents/files,
- current process description,
- stakeholder interviews,
- systems overview,
- historical attempts and vendor offers,
- volume/frequency data.

### Activities

- review inputs,
- interview process users,
- map current state,
- identify decision rules and exceptions,
- quantify effort/waste where possible,
- identify automation candidates,
- recommend first pilot,
- document risks and dependencies.

### Outputs

- current-state map,
- pain-point list,
- process metrics,
- data readiness assessment,
- target-state sketch,
- pilot recommendation,
- assumptions and risks,
- next-step pricing path.

## 4. Audit Questions

### Process

- What triggers the process?
- Who touches it first?
- What happens step by step?
- Which steps are manual?
- Where does work wait?
- Who approves or rejects?
- Where does the output go?

### Data

- What files/documents start the process?
- Are they structured or scanned?
- Which fields matter?
- Which fields are unreliable?
- What is the source of truth?
- Can samples be anonymized?

### Rules

- How are items routed?
- Which rules are explicit?
- Which rules live in people's heads?
- What are the common exceptions?
- Who resolves exceptions?

### Value

- What volume occurs per month/year?
- How much time does one item take?
- How many people are involved?
- What happens when it is wrong or late?
- What would a useful first improvement look like?

## 5. Audit Quality Bar

The audit is not complete until it answers:

- What is the real problem?
- Who owns it?
- What data exists?
- What should not be automated yet?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- What first pilot is narrow enough to succeed?
- What decision can the client make after reading the audit?
